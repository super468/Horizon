Although 314 MEPs voted against it (more than the 276 who supported it), the motion to reject failed to achieve the required absolute majority of 361 votes due to procedural rules. This legislation affects millions of EU citizens' privacy by permitting mass scanning of private communications. The vote occurred on the last day before summer break, with many MEPs already returned to their home countries. The scanning mandate applies to direct messages and emails, while public social media posts and cloud storage were already scannable without this law.

The legislation aims to prevent child sexual abuse material (CSAM) but critics argue it creates mass surveillance infrastructure that could be expanded beyond its original purpose. The EU Parliament had previously rejected this measure twice in March 2024, but the ruling coalition used procedural rules to bring it to a vote again.

## [No Leap Second Will Be Added in December 2026](https://datacenter.iers.org/data/latestVersion/bulletinC.txt) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS) announced that no leap second will be introduced at the end of December 2026, meaning the UTC-TAI offset will remain at -37 seconds. This announcement affects timekeeping systems worldwide, particularly those relying on precise UTC-GPS and UTC-TAI offset calculations for satellite navigation, telecommunications, and financial transactions. Systems in maintenance mode that depend on precise time calculations need this advance notice. TAI (International Atomic Time) and GPS maintain a constant 19-second offset from each other, so if UTC-TAI remains at -37 seconds, then the UTC-GPS offset also stays at -18 seconds. The IERS Bulletin C provides this advance notice to allow time system operators to prepare.

hackernews · ChrisArchitect · Jul 9, 14:16

**Background**: A leap second is added to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) when Earth's rotation, measured by UT1, falls out of sync with International Atomic Time (TAI). The IERS monitors Earth's rotation and is responsible for announcing leap second insertions. TAI is an extremely precise time standard based on atomic clocks that would only lose about one second in 100 million years, while Earth's rotation is affected by various unpredictable factors.
