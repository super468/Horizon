Researchers at the University of Minnesota created SpudCell, the first synthetic cell built entirely from scratch that can both grow and divide. Unlike previous synthetic cells that could only replicate DNA, this breakthrough enables the complete cell division cycle. This marks a historic milestone in synthetic biology, bringing scientists significantly closer to creating truly synthetic life. The achievement could revolutionize bioengineering, drug development, and our fundamental understanding of what constitutes life itself. The team, led by Kate Adamala, achieved cell division by abandoning the natural cytoskeleton approach that traditional cells use. They borrowed genes from a virus and E. coli to construct the synthetic cell. The work was initially rejected by Cell journal after one reviewer claimed SpudCells were not "real biology."

hackernews · defrost · Jul 1, 14:20

**Background**: Synthetic biology aims to create living cells from non-living chemical components. While researchers had previously created synthetic cells capable of DNA replication and metabolism, achieving cell division remained an unsolved challenge. Natural cells use their cytoskeleton—a network of protein fibers—to reorganize and physically split into two daughter cells. SpudCell represents the first successful workaround to this complex biological process.

## [FFmpeg 9.1 Introduces New AAC Encoder](https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,129691.0.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

FFmpeg 9.1 has released a new AAC encoder that improves quality over the previous implementation. The community discussion highlights that while this is a valuable improvement, Opus still outperforms all AAC encoders even at low bitrates like 64 kbps. This matters because FFmpeg's previous AAC encoder produced poor quality output with irritating chirping artifacts, forcing users to install Apple's Core Audio encoder for decent results. The new encoder addresses these quality issues and provides an open-source alternative. The new encoder has two significant limitations: it only supports CBR (Constant Bitrate) encoding and is optimized only for 48kHz sampling. Additionally, during development, a PNS (Perceptual Noise Substitution) bug was discovered in FFmpeg's AAC decoder that had existed for approximately 20 years.

hackernews · ledoge · Jul 1, 14:10

**Background**: FFmpeg is a widely-used open-source multimedia framework that handles audio and video encoding/decoding. AAC (Advanced Audio Coding) is a popular audio compression format that provides better quality than MP3 at similar bitrates. The previous FFmpeg AAC encoder was known for poor quality output with artifacts, while Opus is a more modern open-source audio codec that generally outperforms AAC at lower bitrates.

**Discussion**: The community welcomes the improvement but notes important caveats. Users appreciate the better quality but criticize the CBR-only limitation and lack of 48kHz optimization. Some argue 48kHz is now the standard anyway. There's also appreciation for the PNS bug discovery that was lurking for 20 years.

## [IPFS Content Publishing 10x Faster with Optimistic Provide](https://probelab.io/blog/optimistic-provide/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Probelab engineers optimized IPFS content publishing by making PUT RPCs asynchronous and returning control to users before all confirmations complete, achieving approximately 10x speedup from 13-20 seconds to under 1 second. This optimization significantly improves the real-time publishing capability of IPFS, enabling content to be published almost instantly after being pushed to the network. This addresses a major pain point that has hindered IPFS adoption in production environments. The optimization uses two key techniques: making PUT RPCs asynchronous and estimating total network size using a lightweight method that piggybacks on the routing table refresh mechanism without additional networking overhead. Content providers continue to announce to remaining peers in the background after control is returned.

hackernews · dennis-tra · Jul 1, 15:30

**Background**: IPFS (InterPlanetary File System) is a distributed peer-to-peer file sharing network that uses a Distributed Hash Table (DHT) to locate content. Content publishing in IPFS involves providers announcing content availability to DHT peers through PUT RPCs. Previously, this process was synchronous and waited for all confirmations before returning, causing significant latency.
